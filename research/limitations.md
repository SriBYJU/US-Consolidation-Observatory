# Limitations and Claim Boundaries

1. **BDS is not an M&A database.** The public release measures business dynamics; acquisition-event forecasting is not validated yet.
2. **Structural Readiness Score is exploratory.** It is a transparent descriptive summary, not a causal estimate, expected-return model, or buy/sell recommendation.
3. **Fragmentation is proxied.** Firms per 1,000 employees is not equivalent to revenue concentration, market share, HHI, or addressable acquisition count.
4. **Sector detail is broad.** The current browser experience uses 19 NAICS sectors. Many real acquisition theses operate at narrower 4–6 digit NAICS levels.
5. **Public data arrive with lags.** BDS 2023 is the latest BDS release used by this build.
6. **Suppression exists.** Some agency values are suppressed for disclosure protection; they remain missing.
7. **Classification and measurement can change over time.** Long panels may contain effects from economic classification, methodology, and revisions.
8. **Holdout years include pandemic-era disruption.** The 2020–2021 feature years and 2022–2023 outcomes are economically unusual; this makes the holdout demanding but not necessarily representative of all periods.
9. **The validation target is establishment growth.** Good performance on this target would not imply good performance on acquisitions or returns.
10. **No company-level diligence is performed.** The system does not assess valuation, financial statements, leverage, ownership willingness, legal risk, management, or transaction terms.
11. **Public transaction announcements are selected.** Future transaction-corpus work must explicitly study publication/coverage bias.
12. **External adoption is not a model metric.** Professional usage/reviewer feedback must be documented as real events, never inferred or invented.
