# U.S. Consolidation Observatory — Practitioner Brief
## Technical Platform Brief v0.2

### What this tool can do today

The Observatory provides a transparent state-by-sector view of firm counts, establishment counts, entry, exit, job creation, and business dynamism using official Census BDS data. It can help a researcher identify structurally unusual markets worth investigating further.

### What it cannot do today

It cannot tell a buyer which company to acquire, what price to pay, or whether an industry will produce attractive investment returns. Its exploratory Structural Readiness Score has not yet been validated against transaction-level acquisition outcomes.

### Why the holdout matters

The research framework predicts a future observable outcome using only earlier information and reserves later years for a locked test. On the current establishment-growth task, the selected nonlinear model improves holdout MAE from 2.110 to 1.887 relative to a persistence baseline, while rank correlation remains only 0.380. That mixed result is exactly why the project publishes multiple metrics and failure analysis.

### What v0.2 adds

The platform now includes a three-market comparator, transparent threshold screener, per-market score-robustness intervals, historical replay against realized future establishment growth, rolling-origin diagnostics, and source-attributed evidence-brief export. These are practitioner workflow tools, not investment recommendations.

### How to use the explorer responsibly

Use high-scoring markets as **questions**, not answers. Investigate why a market ranks highly, inspect the component data, review time trends, and then perform company-level and transaction-level diligence outside this tool.

### Current research signal

In a post-hoc historical replay, the within-sector SRS top quintile showed higher subsequent two-year establishment growth than the remainder in 43 of 44 annual cohorts from 1978–2021. The fixed HGB specification also beat persistence across four rolling-origin windows on all three reported metrics. Both findings concern establishment growth, not acquisitions.

### Next research milestone

The next high-value step is building and independently checking a transaction-event dataset so the central consolidation hypothesis can be tested directly.
